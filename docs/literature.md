# Where this Article sits

Three bodies of work bear on the governance of stratospheric aerosol injection. Risk-risk analysis identifies what
a comparison must include, and leaves the authority to decide unassigned. The literature on termination shows that
the danger of stopping depends on design, and relies on capable states standing ready to maintain or resume an
intervention; it has no account of what follows when exposed parties withdraw their consent. A third literature
asks whether solar geoengineering can be reconciled with democratic control at all. The Article takes that last
question as its own and answers it institutionally, with two duties and the legal precedents for each. This page
records what each body of authority settles, what it leaves open, and what the Article adds. The complete list of
211 authorities and sources is in the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

## Authorities by weight

| Weight | Sources | What they settle | What they leave open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **International decisions** | *Pulp Mills*; *Certain Activities*; ITLOS Advisory Opinion (2024); ICJ Advisory Opinion on climate change (2025) | Environmental impact assessment, notification and consultation; stringent due diligence | An adjudicated conclusion about deployment |

| **Scholarship and science** | Risk-risk analysis; termination studies; the democracy debate; impact modeling | Method and evidence | Who may choose |

## What the Article adds

| Body of work | Where it stops | The Article's step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk-risk analysis: Graham and Wiener; Felgenhauer et al. | It lists what a comparison includes | A comparative justification duty: the proponent defends its comparator and exposes its distributional assumptions to challenge by those exposed |
| Termination studies: Parker and Irvine | Termination shock is avoidable if capable states stand ready | A continuity duty that separates political control from physical operation, so that a refusal changes who operates and starts a reviewed taper |
| The democracy debate: Szerszynski et al.; Horton et al. | Whether democratic control is possible | An institutional answer: separate concurrence of exposed constituencies, with precedents in the Montreal Protocol and in reorganization law |
| Voting proposals: Weitzman | A supermajority to restrain the actor most eager to cool | The majority must include those who would bear the exposure |
| Liability proposals: Horton and Keith | Parametric insurance paid on a climate index | Pre-funded assistance as a condition of authorization, with a separate liability track |
| Existing international law | Care, assessment and consultation | The point of departure is stated: under the prevention articles the state of origin may still authorize, and under the proposed duty it may not |

## Key authorities

### Risk-risk analysis

<a id="graham1995"></a>
**Risk vs. Risk: Tradeoffs in Protecting Health and the Environment (John D. Graham & Jonathan Baert Wiener eds., 1995).**
The taxonomy of countervailing risks: offsets, substitutions, transfers and transformations. The framework
identifies what a decision must compare. Two of its categories are now positive law in article 195 of the Law of
the Sea Convention, which the Article shows, and neither the taxonomy nor the treaty says who decides whether a
transfer is justified.

<a id="felgenhauer2022"></a>
**Tyler Felgenhauer et al., Solar Radiation Modification: A Risk-Risk Analysis (Carnegie Climate Governance Initiative 2022).**
Each policy option is scored on benefits, costs, countervailing risks and distribution. It is the fullest
application of the method to this subject, and it treats the choice among options as the reader's. The Article
starts where it stops.

### Evidence

<a id="irvine2020"></a>
**Peter J. Irvine & David W. Keith, *Halving Warming with Stratospheric Aerosol Geoengineering Moderates Policy-Relevant Climate Hazards*, 15 Env't Rsch. Letters 044011 (2020).**
Moderate cooling reduces several climate hazards in most regions in the models studied. The Article takes this
favorable evidence seriously and shows what it cannot settle: the welfare metric, the acceptable inequality and
the allocation of authority through which a model result acquires policy significance.

<a id="parker2018"></a>
**Andy Parker & Peter J. Irvine, *The Risk of Termination Shock from Solar Geoengineering*, 6 Earth's Future 456 (2018).**
Termination shock is less likely than often assumed, because gradual withdrawal, limited cooling and redundancy
change the risk, and because capable states could maintain or resume an intervention. The account depends on
those states standing ready. It does not address an authorization that lapses because exposed parties refuse it,
which is the case the continuity duty is built for.

<a id="yuan2024"></a>
**Tianle Yuan et al., *Abrupt Reduction in Shipping Emission as an Inadvertent Geoengineering Termination Shock Produces Substantial Radiative Warming*, 5 Commc'ns Earth & Env't 281 (2024).**
The 2020 limit on sulfur in marine fuel is analyzed as an inadvertent termination of aerosol cooling. A lawful
regulatory decision ended a cooling influence abruptly. The exit paradox has occurred in kind.

<a id="baum2024"></a>
**Chad M. Baum et al., *Public Perceptions and Support of Climate Intervention Technologies Across the Global North and Global South*, 15 Nature Commc'ns 2060 (2024).**
A survey of more than thirty thousand respondents in thirty countries found publics in the Global South more
supportive of climate-intervention technologies than publics in the Global North. A fair account of restraint has
to recognize that preference and the losses that delay causes.

### International law

<a id="unclos195"></a>
**United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea art. 195, Dec. 10, 1982, 1833 U.N.T.S. 397; Request for an Advisory Opinion Submitted by the Commission of Small Island States on Climate Change and International Law, Case No. 31, Advisory Opinion of May 21, 2024 (ITLOS).**
States shall not transfer damage or hazards from one area to another or transform one type of pollution into
another, and the Tribunal has said that marine geoengineering would be contrary to the article if it had that
consequence. Risk transfer and risk transformation are a prohibition here and no longer only analytic labels.

<a id="vienna1985"></a>
**Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer art. 2, Mar. 22, 1985, 1513 U.N.T.S. 293.**
Parties protect against adverse effects of human activities that modify or are likely to modify the ozone layer.
Sulfate injection is such an activity. The convention bears on stratospheric aerosols more directly than the
instruments usually invoked, and it supplies a duty of care, not an authority to refuse.

<a id="ilc2021"></a>
**Int'l Law Comm'n, Draft Guidelines on the Protection of the Atmosphere, guideline 7, U.N. Doc. A/76/10 (2021).**
Intentional large-scale modification of the atmosphere should be conducted only with prudence and caution and
subject to applicable rules, in a commentary that names stratospheric aerosols. This is the most specific
statement in the codification work, and it is hortatory.

<a id="ilc2001"></a>
**Int'l Law Comm'n, Draft Articles on Prevention of Transboundary Harm from Hazardous Activities arts. 9, 10, U.N. Doc. A/56/10 (2001).**
Alternatives and the costs of prevention are factors in an equitable balance of interests, and if consultations
fail the state of origin may still authorize the activity. The Article marks its departure from existing law at
exactly this point.

<a id="pulpmills2010"></a>
**Pulp Mills on the River Uruguay (Arg. v. Uru.), Judgment, 2010 I.C.J. 14 (Apr. 20); Certain Activities Carried Out by Nicaragua in the Border Area (Costa Rica v. Nicar.), Judgment, 2015 I.C.J. 665 (Dec. 16).**
Environmental impact assessment where there is a risk of significant transboundary harm, and notification and
consultation when the assessment confirms it. The duties run between states. They give affected populations no
route to challenge a comparator.

### The two duties and their precedents

<a id="montreal1987"></a>
**Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer art. 10(9), Sept. 16, 1987, 1522 U.N.T.S. 3 (as amended June 29, 1990).**
Decisions on the financial mechanism require a two-thirds majority that includes a majority of the
developing-country parties and a majority of the others. Separate concurrence is working treaty law where one
group's decision binds another with different stakes.

<a id="bankr"></a>
**11 U.S.C. §§ 1126(c), 1129.** [text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1129)
A reorganization plan is accepted class by class, and a dissenting holder in an impaired class receives at least
what liquidation would have paid. The second feature is the structure of the Article's priority rule: a floor
defined against a comparator, which aggregate benefit does not override.

<a id="weitzman2015"></a>
**Martin L. Weitzman, *A Voting Architecture for the Governance of Free-Driver Externalities, with Application to Geoengineering*, 117 Scandinavian J. Econ. 1049 (2015).**
A supermajority rule restrains the actor most eager to cool. The rule sets a threshold of support. It does not ask
whether the majority includes those who would bear the exposure, which separate concurrence does.

<a id="livingwill"></a>
**12 U.S.C. § 5365(d)(1); 10 C.F.R. § 50.75(a); 15 U.S.C. § 717f(b); 12 U.S.C. § 1821(n).**
A large financial company files its plan for orderly resolution in advance. A nuclear licensee shows assurance of
decommissioning funds long before decommissioning. A natural-gas company may not abandon service without
permission. A bridge institution assumes a failed bank's deposits for a bounded term. Domestic law has long
experience with enterprises that cannot be allowed to stop abruptly, and these four instruments correspond to the
four elements of the continuity duty.

<a id="hortonkeith2019"></a>
**Joshua B. Horton & David W. Keith, *Multilateral Parametric Climate Risk Insurance*, 19 Climate Pol'y 820 (2019).**
Insurance paid on a climate index spares a claimant proof that a particular loss was caused by the intervention.
The proposal treats insurance as a tool to reach agreement. The Article makes pre-funded assistance a condition of
authorization and keeps liability on a separate track.

### The debate over research and democratic control

<a id="szerszynski2013"></a>
**Bronislaw Szerszynski et al., *Why Solar Radiation Management Geoengineering and Democracy Won't Mix*, 45 Env't & Plan. A 2809 (2013); Joshua B. Horton et al., *Solar Geoengineering and Democracy*, 18 Glob. Env't Pol. 5 (2018).**
One side argues that the technology is centralizing by nature; the other that nothing in it precludes democratic
governance. Both argue at the level of compatibility. The Article gives the institutions that would have to
exist: a duty to justify, separate concurrence, and continuity.

<a id="biermann2022"></a>
**Frank Biermann et al., *Solar Geoengineering: The Case for an International Non-Use Agreement*, 13 WIREs Climate Change e754 (2022).**
A call for governments to forgo development and deployment. It gives little weight to the costs of restraint. The Article
requires restraining institutions to assess forgone benefits and to say what would change their decision.
