# The proposal as a working instrument

The Article proposes two legal duties, a priority rule and a standard of scrutiny for research. This page sets them
out as the records a review institution would require. Every element comes from the Article's text. The duties are
a proposed architecture: they would be adopted by international agreement and implemented through domestic
authorization of operators.

## 1. Comparative justification record

Required before any deployment that would affect the climate.

| Section | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant | The state or coalition, and the operators to be bound through domestic authorization |
| Specified intervention | The injection strategy: materials, latitudes, altitude, intensity, schedule |
| Comparators | (a) a reference climate period; (b) the future without the intervention, on stated emissions and adaptation assumptions; (c) feasible portfolios: the same mitigation without the intervention, and feasible uses of the proposal's resources for adaptation or added mitigation |
| Two-stage analysis | First, marginal physical effects with emissions and adaptation fixed. Second, whole portfolios with expenditure, political behavior and mitigation allowed to vary |
| Feasibility account | Which constraints are physical, which institutional, and which could change through the applicant's own decisions |
| Disaggregated exposures | Effects by livelihood, location, health and generation |
| Evidence by mechanism and strength | The established cooling mechanism; regional outcomes that depend on design; uncertain welfare and geopolitical effects |
| Decision record | Model results, disputed assumptions, unquantified hazards and explicit evaluative weights, kept separate |
| Objections and answers | The strongest supported objections, and the protection or remedy offered for each residual burden |
| Funded responsibility | Independent monitoring; adaptation, emergency-response and claims resources committed before deployment; liability for attributable harm kept separate from assistance to highly exposed groups |

## 2. Review and challenge

| Element | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Independent science panel | Assesses the evidence |
| Review council | Makes the policy decision and publishes responses to material objections, with transparent appointment and recusal |
| Exposed-constituency representatives | Selected independently of applicants, with collective power to **withhold approval or require redesign** through separate concurrence of sponsor and exposed-constituency representatives |
| Participation support | Publicly funded and independently administered, with the constituency's own choice of experts, access to the record and a funded opportunity to propose alternatives |
| Designated representation | Future generations and ecological interests, with the representation open to contest |
| Bounds | Published scoping criteria, representative submissions and bounded review periods |

**Separate concurrence is an established device.**

| Precedent | Rule | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Montreal Protocol art. 10(9) | Decisions on the financial mechanism need a two-thirds majority that also includes a majority of the developing-country parties and a majority of the others | Neither group can be outvoted as a group |
| 11 U.S.C. § 1126(c) | A reorganization plan is accepted class by class, by two-thirds in amount and more than one-half in number of the claims voting in each class | Consent is counted where the stakes differ |
| 11 U.S.C. § 1129(a)(7) | A dissenting holder in an impaired class receives at least what liquidation would have paid | A floor defined against a baseline that the majority cannot vote away |

## 3. Priority rule

```text
Is there a credible prospect of a grave, concentrated increase in harm
relative to the defended feasible comparator?
   no  ──► ordinary comparative justification
   yes ──► strong presumption against approval, rebuttable only if ALL hold:
             (1) the intervention averts at least comparably grave threats to other vulnerable groups
             (2) no feasible less harmful portfolio could do so
             (3) shared authorization and credible relief are secured
           Aggregated lesser benefits elsewhere do not rebut the presumption.
```

The comparison that counts is whether a group is worse off than under an available alternative, and not whether it
is worse off than in a historical climate. A severely exposed group may rationally support the intervention
despite residual losses, and outside advocates do not overwrite its position.

## 4. Continuity plan

A condition of authorization, filed before the first injection.

| Requirement | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Three decisions kept separate: who governs, who operates, what trajectory | Political control can change without an automatic physical stop |
| A bounded transition authority, designated collectively before deployment | Interim operation after approval is withdrawn, without renewing the rejected operator's mandate |
| Operational redundancy; successor access to information and equipment | A successor can actually operate |
| Independent monitoring | Decisions are tied to measured conditions |
| Transition finance that does not make continued injection the only funded option | Budget design does not prejudge review |
| Scenarios evaluated: the sponsor withdraws, funding fails, the preferred mitigation path is not achieved | Continuity is tested against its failure modes |
| A tapering pathway evaluated against emissions and atmospheric conditions | A political end starts a gradual withdrawal |
| Independent review of continuation, redesign and tapering | Continuity carries no entitlement to the original dose |

**Each element has a counterpart in law that already governs systems that cannot safely stop.**

| Element of the continuity duty | Existing instrument | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A continuity plan filed before operation | The resolution plan of a large financial company | 12 U.S.C. § 5365(d)(1) |
| Financing committed before it is needed | Decommissioning funding assurance for a nuclear licensee | 10 C.F.R. § 50.75(a) |
| An exit that requires permission | Abandonment of natural-gas facilities or service only with the Commission's approval | 15 U.S.C. § 717f(b) |
| An interim successor with a bounded mandate | The bridge institution that assumes a failed bank's deposits | 12 U.S.C. § 1821(n) |

Carbon removal counts as an exit asset once it exists.

## 5. Research scrutiny

- Activities are classified by **expected effects and cumulative scale**. A test designed to alter the climate
  faces deployment review.
- Smaller activities receive proportionate scrutiny of releases, location, duration and links to a larger program.
  Related projects are reviewed together, and the classification is published.
- Inquiry is permitted when its informational value, alternatives, direct effects and contribution to a program
  withstand review. Experiments that mainly advance deployability, without answering a decision-relevant question
  that safer means cannot answer, are refused.
- Public funding and domestic permission are conditional on transparent records, independent review and a bar on
  progression to deployment without further authorization. Investigation of reasons against deployment is funded,
  as is expertise chosen by potential host and affected communities.

## 6. Duties of restraint

Institutions that maintain a moratorium periodically assess forgone benefits, residual climate damage and progress
on alternative protections. They state what evidence or institutional developments would change their decision,
and they reconsider earlier when material evidence changes.
